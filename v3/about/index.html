01 / PracticeProcedural · Generative · Physical

Creating tools that support a designers process instead of fighting it

Joe is an expert in design and manufacturing with a special expertise in procedural and generative design, combining an artist’s eye for form with an engineer’s command of systems. His deep fluency with Blender, Geometry Nodes, and computational design connects ambitious creative ideas to the tools and techniques that make them possible. He builds the logic behind the form: reusable modeling systems that turn a set of rules into geometry people can explore, adapt, and make their own.

His design consultancy, The Well, works across computational design, procedural workflows, and 3D visualization.

Through NO3D Tools, Joe develops procedural Blender tools for modeling, drawing, and fabrication. Projects range from generators for bolts and brackets to Chrome Crayon, a tool for turning drawn curves into 3D forms. These systems make complex geometry available through controls that people can explore and manipulate.

02 / Approach

A recurring concern in his work is how tools shape the experience of making. He develops ideas by building, testing, and refining them in use. That approach extends from physical objects, such as a desk-mounted caliper holder, to software that reduces the steps between modeling a part and preparing it for printing. Naming files, moving between applications, and finding reusable work become design problems in their own right.

03 / Storytelling & teaching

Social media · Conferences · Team training

An accomplished storyteller and educator, Joe turns complex design techniques into clear, compelling stories. He brings the same technical depth and creative energy to social media, conference talks, and team training, making procedural and generative design approachable without losing its richness. His strength is giving people both the understanding and the excitement to experiment: how a system works, why the decisions matter, and what they can make with it.

Joe also documents the process behind his work: the experiments, failed approaches, and discoveries that lead to a useful result. His Proof of Life project brings that interest into writing, pairing an Obsidian plugin with a web player that lets readers watch a text develop through its edits. Across these projects, he is interested in making creative processes more accessible while preserving the attention, curiosity, and human effort they require.
